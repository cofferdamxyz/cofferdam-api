# Embedding models for the token registry, 2026-10-25

Which encoder should `work-embed` use? Today it is bge-m3. This report compares it with the other embedding models Workers AI hosts, and with two domain models from TechWolf, on [WorkRB](https://github.com/techwolf-ai/workrb) 0.6.0 (TechWolf's work-domain benchmark, Apache-2.0). It adds three checks of our own: a reranker stage, our live-test failure strings, and the registry eval.

## Models

| Model | Where it ran | How it was called |
|---|---|---|
| `@cf/baai/bge-m3` (today's pin) | Workers AI REST | plain text, as `cofferdam-api` sends it |
| `@cf/google/embeddinggemma-300m` | Workers AI REST | its card's retrieval prompts (`task: search result \| query: `, `title: none \| text: `) |
| `@cf/qwen/qwen3-embedding-0.6b` | Workers AI REST | queries with a task instruction, documents without |
| `TechWolf/ConTeXT-Skill-Extraction-base` | the Mac's CPU, sentence-transformers | WorkRB's `ConTeXTMatchModel` |
| `TechWolf/JobBERT-v3` (MIT) | the Mac's CPU, sentence-transformers | WorkRB's `JobBERTModel` (title and skill branches) |

The hosted models were reached through an adapter that caches every embedding. It and the run scripts are in [`workrb/`](workrb/), as they ran.

## Tasks (test splits)

| Task | What a query is | Languages | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Occupation → skills (`ESCOJob2SkillRanking`, ESCO 1.2.0 relations) | an ESCO occupation name; its essential and optional skills are the answers | en, es, pt | about 3,030 queries × 13,939 skills a language |
| National title → occupation (`MELORanking`, Avature's MELO) | a national occupation name (US, Spain, Portugal) | en, es, pt, monolingual | 633, 1,580 and 379 queries |
| Vacancy title → occupation (`JobBERTJobNormRanking`) | an English vacancy title | en | 15,173 queries × 2,942 occupations |
| Job-ad sentence → skills (`HouseSkillExtractRanking`, `TechSkillExtractRanking`) | an English sentence from a job ad | en | 262 and 338 queries × 13,891 skills |

Metrics: MAP (mean average precision) and hit@1 (the top answer is right), in %.

## Results

| Task | bge-m3 | EmbeddingGemma | Qwen3-0.6B | ConTeXT | JobBERT-v3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Occupation → skills, MAP, en | 11.3 | 13.9 | 8.9 | **14.6** | 14.0 |
| Occupation → skills, MAP, es | 8.9 | **12.0** | 8.9 | 6.4 | 11.6 |
| Occupation → skills, MAP, pt | 7.9 | **11.0** | 8.6 | 5.9 | 10.8 |
| National title → occupation, hit@1, US | 43.0 | **60.5** | 32.2 | 58.6 | 54.5 |
| National title → occupation, hit@1, Spain | 29.6 | **39.6** | 20.4 | 37.5 | 21.3 |
| National title → occupation, hit@1, Portugal | 54.6 | **57.5** | 31.7 | 50.4 | 51.5 |
| Vacancy title → occupation, MAP, en | 33.9 | **37.0** | 28.5 | 36.7 | 35.4 |
| Job-ad sentence → skills, MAP, House, en | 23.3 | 28.8 | 24.6 | **42.3** | 15.6 |
| Job-ad sentence → skills, MAP, Tech, en | 32.6 | 37.7 | 35.1 | **53.6** | 29.9 |

- **EmbeddingGemma leads every task in Spanish and Portuguese, and every English task but sentence extraction.** Workers AI hosts it, with 768 dimensions to bge-m3's 1,024.
- **ConTeXT is the English sentence specialist,** 14 to 16 MAP points ahead of every other model there. It falls below bge-m3 in Spanish and Portuguese, is not hosted on Workers AI, and its model card states no licence.
- **JobBERT-v3 is a title model.** It is close to EmbeddingGemma on occupation → skills in Spanish and Portuguese, and weak on sentence extraction and Spain's titles.
- **Qwen3-0.6B trails EmbeddingGemma everywhere.**
- **Our English EmbeddingGemma and ConTeXT numbers match the [WorkBench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07969)'s within about a point** (title → occupation 37.0 against 36.7; Tech extraction 53.6 against 53.6 for ConTeXT), which checks the harness. bge-m3 is in neither the paper nor the 2026 challenge repositories, so these are the first numbers for it on these tasks that we found.

## A reranker after bge-m3

Workers AI's `@cf/baai/bge-reranker-base` rescored bge-m3's top 30 for each query, on a smaller task set (6,231 calls):

| Task | bge-m3 | bge-m3 + reranker |
|---|---|---|
| Occupation → skills, MAP, en | 11.3 | 10.6 |
| National title → occupation, hit@1, US / Spain / Portugal | 43.0 / 29.6 / 54.6 | 49.0 / 25.3 / 46.4 |
| Job-ad sentence → skills, MAP, House / Tech, en | 23.3 / 32.6 | 28.7 / 31.4 |

It helps a little in English and hurts in Spanish and Portuguese. The model was trained on English and Chinese, and the multilingual reranker of its family is not on Workers AI. Clef's choice stays the second stage.

## The registry eval on EmbeddingGemma

[`registry-2026-10-25-tau-1-0.01.md`](registry-2026-10-25-tau-1-0.01.md) and [`registry-2026-10-25-embeddinggemma-300m-tau-1-0.01.md`](registry-2026-10-25-embeddinggemma-300m-tau-1-0.01.md) set τ_auto 1 and τ_low 0.01, so every string goes through retrieval and Clef: bge-m3 is right on 101 of 106 strings, EmbeddingGemma on 102 (no wrong token). That is on the 50-token synthetic seed, and without EmbeddingGemma's prompts, which `embedTexts` does not send. Its first calls from the Mac outlasted the op's 10 s, so the eval retries.

## Cost and reproduction

Workers AI calls: bge-m3 1,290, EmbeddingGemma 1,290, Qwen3 4,017 (it takes 32 texts a call), the reranker 6,231; a few cents in all. The TechWolf models ran on the CPU in 8 to 12 minutes each. The bench lived in `~/Development/workrb-bench` (Python 3.12 through `uv`, WorkRB 0.6.0, `HF_HOME` inside it). Two lessons from the setup:
- Fetch the Hugging Face datasets once and load them locally, because anonymous API calls get rate-limited (HTTP 429).
- Keep ESCO's CSVs out of `~/Library/Caches`, which macOS empties when the disk runs low.

Datasets: MELO (Avature), the JobBERT evaluation set and TechWolf's skill-extraction sets, under their own licences (MIT, CC BY 4.0), downloaded at run time and never committed.
